## 4. Gap recommendations

### 4a. Volume pricing: the contractor with 11 permits
**What the market publishes:**
- No South Florida expediter publishes a volume discount or retainer price. All of them quote it privately.
- Permit Place: per-project cost falls at **20+ locations/year**, no percentage given ([link](https://permitplace.com/compare/permitplace-vs-permitflow/)).
- PermitFlow: volume-tiered discounts with a minimum monthly fee, no figures published ([link](https://softwarefinder.com/construction/permitflow)).
- Permit Runner (CA): "bulk permits: quoted per project" ([PDF](https://pronline.permitrunner.net/2024permitrunnerfeeschedule.pdf)).
- Mobile American Notary: contract rate for frequent users ([link](https://www.mobileamericannotary.com/services/permit-running.html)).
- The only published discount figure I found for a comparable solo service business is Eco-Office Gals at **$70 → $60/hr (about 14%)** for a 10+ hr/month commitment ([link](https://eco-officegals.com/services-fees/virtual-assitant-service-hours/)).
- One vendor argues that subscriptions "punish" seasonal contractors, and that per-project pricing matches cost to activity ([iPermit USA](https://www.ipermitusa.com/ipermit-blog/best-permit-expediting-services-hvac-contractors)).

**[INFERRED] Recommendation.** Use volume tiers, not a pure subscription. The discount applies to base permit fees only, and add-ons stay at list price.

| Permits opened in a calendar month (per client) | Discount on base permit fees |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | list price |
| 5–9 | 5% |
| 10–19 | **10%** |
| 20+ | 15% (or custom quote) |

- **For the 11-permit contractor:** apply **10%**. If they're mostly small permits, that's about $405 each instead of $450. Put it in writing as a "Volume Partner" rate. Review it quarterly, and drop them back a tier if volume falls.
- **Optional roofer/restoration retainer [INFERRED]:** a **"10-Permit Monthly Plan": $3,900/month billed in advance**. That covers up to 10 small-tier building permits (effectively $390 each, about 13% off, in line with the 14% retainer discount above). Extra permits are $400 each, and unused permits roll over one month only. It replaces the 50/50 deposit terms for plan clients and smooths her cash flow.
- The 15% ceiling is my judgment, set so the 20+ tier still clears her own cost well above the $12.76–$23.49/hr employee wage floor (section 1e).

### 4b. General administrative work for the contractor
**Market evidence:**
- **Employee floor:** construction admin in Miami averages **$20.12/hr**, and permit coordinators in Miami average **$23.49/hr** (section 1e).
- **1099 loading:** a contractor pays both halves of Social Security and Medicare (**15.3%** self-employment tax, [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes)), plus her own software, insurance, unpaid admin time and no benefits. That's why freelance rates run well above wages.
- **Freelance rates:**
  - US general-admin VAs charge **$25–$35/hr**.
  - Specialized PM, EA or bookkeeping VAs charge **$40–$75/hr** ([Arma Sourcing](https://armasourcing.com/how-much-does-it-actually-cost-to-hire-a-virtual-assistant-in-2026/)).
  - A US VA firm lists **$70/hr, or $60/hr** on a 10+ hr retainer ([Eco-Office Gals](https://eco-officegals.com/services-fees/virtual-assitant-service-hours/)).
  - Offshore-heavy Upwork admin pools have a median of about $14/hr. That's not a relevant comparison for a local, licensed-process specialist.

**[INFERRED] Recommendation:**

| Line | Rate | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| General construction admin (scheduling, doc control, vendor/sub follow-up, data entry, filing) | **$45/hr** | Above the $25–$35 generic US VA band because she brings construction and permitting context. About 2× the Miami construction-admin wage of $20.12, which covers 1099 costs. |
| Admin retainer block (prepaid, 10+ hrs/month) | **$40/hr** | About an 11% retainer discount, in line with Eco-Office Gals' 14%. |
| Permit / code consulting, comment resolution, expired-permit research, meetings with the building department | **$65/hr** | Within the specialized $40–$75 VA band, the Miami expediter $50–$200 band, and the $75–$150 meeting rate ([CostOwl](https://www.costowl.com/home-improvement/builders/home-building-permit-cost/)). This is also the hourly option for heavy comment responses. |

- **Billing increments:** **15-minute increments** with a **1-hour minimum per request or day** for ad-hoc (non-retainer) work, matching Eco-Office Gals. Retainer clients are billed in 15-minute increments against their block with no per-task minimum. Unused retainer hours expire at the end of the month (or roll over one month, her choice).
- Log admin time separately from flat-fee permit work so the flat fees don't get eroded.

## 6. How to present pricing on the website
1. **Show "starting at" prices for the three building tiers and trade permits.** Permits By Mack and Permit CPR publish "starting at" figures ([Mack](https://permitrunningservices.com/roofing-permit-services/), [CPR](https://www.permitcpr.com/Fees)), while most South Florida competitors are quote-only. Published prices are a differentiator for small contractors who want to know the cost before they call.
2. **Say what's included in every flat fee:** BPA and NOC prep, submission, city communication, tracking, and 2 comment rounds. The JDJ Miami guide tells buyers to confirm "whether the fee covers all submissions, follow-ups, and document handling" ([JDJ](https://jdj-consulting.com/guide-on-hiring-a-building-permit-expediter-miami-florida/)), so answer that on the page.
3. **State plainly that government fees are separate:** "City/county permit and recording fees are passed through at cost with receipts."
4. **Make large, expired-permit and volume work "quote after review"**, the way ClosePermits.com and Sarasota Permits handle close-outs.
5. **Add a "Contractors pulling 5+ permits a month" call-to-action** for volume pricing. Don't publish the tier percentages, so she can negotiate.
6. **Notary lines must use the statutory wording**, "per notarial act," with travel and after-hours fees "agreed in advance" ([Fla. Stat. ch. 117](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/Chapter117/All)).
7. **Compliance check before launch:** the City of Miami requires permit expediters to be registered with the city before acting for contractors or owners ([PermitFlow Miami guide](https://www.permitflow.com/blog/miami-permit-expediter)). Confirm her registration status in each jurisdiction she lists on the site.
